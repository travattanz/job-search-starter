# Writing that reads human

Applies to every free-text thing written for an application: cover letters, "why
this job" answers, screening free-text, recruiter and follow-up messages, and any
resume bullet rewritten for a posting.

**Read `Career/voice.md` alongside this file.** That one records how this
specific person writes, taken from their own messages. This one is the general
method. When the two disagree, their voice wins.

The goal is text that reads like they wrote it — practical, specific, uneven.
That's also what scores human on a detector. Chasing a detector score directly
produces worse writing; writing like a person fixes both.

## Words and phrases to never use

These run through generated text and almost never appear in real writing:

delve, leverage (as a verb), utilize, robust, seamless, spearheaded, pivotal,
testament, tapestry, landscape (figurative), realm, underscore, foster, cultivate,
myriad, plethora, navigate (figurative), resonate, align with (as filler),
passionate about, excited for this opportunity, I am writing to express, thrilled,
honored, dynamic, innovative, cutting-edge, state-of-the-art, holistic, synergy,
elevate, unlock, empower, meticulous, comprehensive, ensure (use "make sure"),
furthermore, moreover, additionally (as a paragraph opener), in today's fast-paced
world, it is worth noting, that said (as a tic), "not only X but also Y", "it
isn't just X, it's Y".

## Structural tells

- **Threes.** Three-item lists, three-clause sentences, three paragraphs. Break
  it: use two, use four, use one.
- **Uniform rhythm.** Every sentence 15–25 words. Real writing puts a four-word
  sentence next to a thirty-word one.
- **Em-dashes everywhere.** Most people use zero in their entire sent mail. Check
  `voice.md` for the count and match it. Commas, periods, parentheses.
- **Perfect parallelism.** A bullet list with identical grammar in every line
  reads machine-made. Let one break form.
- **The wrap-up paragraph** that restates the opening. Cut it and end on the last
  real point.
- **Rhetorical question openers.** "What makes a good engineer?" Never.
- **Bolded key terms** sprinkled through prose.
- **Hedging pairs.** "While X, it is also true that Y."

## What to do instead

- Open with a fact, not a feeling. A number, a piece of equipment, a place, a
  date. Specificity is the hardest thing to fake and the clearest sign of a real
  writer.
- Use contractions: I've, didn't, it's, that's.
- Let one sentence run long, or land a little rough. Uniform smoothness is the
  tell.
- Start a sentence with And, But or So once in a while.
- Plain verbs: built, ran, fixed, wired, checked, sized, drew up, walked the site.
- First person, active voice, past tense for work already done.
- Say why it matters, in one short practical reason.
- Name the specific thing in the posting the answer responds to, pulled from
  `posting.md`. Not the company's mission statement — the actual requirement.
- Keep their register: whatever `voice.md` says. Never add salesmanship they
  wouldn't use.

## Self-check — run before anything is pasted into a form

All of these must pass:

1. Read it as if saying it out loud. Rewrite any sentence you wouldn't say.
2. Banned-word count: zero.
3. Em-dashes: match `voice.md`, which usually means zero.
4. In any answer longer than one paragraph, at least one sentence under 8 words
   and one over 25.
5. At least one concrete detail per paragraph — a number, tool, standard,
   employer, or place.
6. Delete any sentence that loses nothing when removed. If several go, it was
   padding.
7. Swap in a different company name. If the paragraph still works, it's generic —
   rewrite it around something only this posting says.
8. No sentence claims anything outside the ✅ ledger in `skills.md`.
9. Read `voice.md` back against the draft. Would they have written these
   sentences?

## Reality check

No detector is reliable. GPTZero, Turnitin and Originality all flag human writing,
and their scores shift between versions. Never tell anyone a piece of text is "95%
human" or that it will pass a named tool, because there's no way to know that. Say
the self-check passed, and flag anything that felt forced.
